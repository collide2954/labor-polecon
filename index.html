How to read these numbers

  • Rebuilt weekly. BLS, BEA, Census, Federal Reserve and lobbying figures are pulled from the agencies’ own files every Sunday. Tap a mark for its numbers.
  • Classical terms. Variable capital is the pay of production and nonsupervisory workers outside trade, finance and real estate. Surplus value is the rest of net value added, for nonfinancial corporations, and includes the pay of supervisors, managers, officers and trade staff. Each term’s definition and source line is in the table at the end.
  • Annual values. Yearly figures are BLS annual averages and BEA annual totals. Where a single month is shown, it says so.
  • Latest-year dollars. Where a chart says so, dollars from earlier years are restated in the latest full year’s prices with the CPI-W, the price index for urban wage earners and clerical workers.
  • Fixed tables. Tables marked Historical Statistics are typed from the 1975 Census volume, with its series numbers. They do not change.
  • Breaks in series. Old and new series measure different things. They are drawn as separate lines and never joined.
  • Named groups only. Lobbying totals cover the organizations listed, reporting their own lobbying. Firms they hire are not counted.
